# AT27C256R color PROM

Used in Tiers A, B, and C.

Each programmed part holds the same 64-byte kit, `firmware/a/at27c256r_kit.bin`. One chip sits in the socket at a time. The second chip uses the same wires and the same table.

The package is a 28-pin PDIP with 0.6 inch (600 mil) between the pin rows. The center channel of one breadboard is 0.3 inch, so the two rows do not land on opposite sides of that channel. A 28-pin socket of the same width, with one jumper per pin, keeps the pin numbers below attached to the right holes. Pin 1 is the pin next to the notch. Down the notched side the pins are 1 through 14. Up the far side the pins are 15 through 28.

Power is off while a chip goes into the socket and while a chip comes out. Pin 1 (VPP) and pin 28 (VCC) share the same 5 V rail, so they rise and fall together.

## Power

| Pin | Name | Wire |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 28 | VCC | Breadboard +5 V |
| 14 | GND | Breadboard ground |
| 1 | VPP | Breadboard +5 V |

A 100 nF ceramic capacitor sits from pin 28 to pin 14, with short leads. Pin 1 is the programming-voltage pin. On this board it stays at 5 V. The 12.5 V to 13 V pulse belongs on the T48, during the burn, and does not appear on the breadboard.

## Address pins that stay at ground

The kit occupies addresses 0 through 63. Any of these pins left high, or left open, selects some other address. Those locations are still `0xFF`.

| Pin | Name | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | A12 | Address bit 12. Tie to ground. |
| 3 | A7 | Address bit 7. Tie to ground. |
| 4 | A6 | Address bit 6. Tie to ground. |
| 21 | A10 | Address bit 10. Tie to ground. |
| 23 | A11 | Address bit 11. Tie to ground. |
| 24 | A9 | Address bit 9. Tie to ground. |
| 25 | A8 | Address bit 8. Tie to ground. |
| 26 | A13 | Address bit 13. Tie to ground. |
| 27 | A14 | Address bit 14. Tie to ground. High selects the upper 16 KB. |

## Enables

Both enables are active low. A high on either one turns the data pins off.

| Pin | Name | Wire for a read | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 20 | CE# | Ground | Chip enable. High forces the outputs off. |
| 22 | OE# | Ground | Output enable. High forces the outputs off. |

## Index switches

Six DIP switches pick the color index. A5 is the 32s bit. A0 is the 1s bit. The index is the binary value of A5 through A0.

Each of these pins gets a 10 kohm resistor to ground, and a switch to +5 V. A closed switch is a 1. An open switch is a 0, held there by the resistor.

| Pin | Name | Weight |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 5 | A5 | 32 |
| 6 | A4 | 16 |
| 7 | A3 | 8 |
| 8 | A2 | 4 |
| 9 | A1 | 2 |
| 10 | A0 | 1 |

## Data LEDs

The byte is packed R3G3B2, `{RRRGGGBB}`. O7 is the heavy red bit. O0 is the light blue bit.

| Pins | Bits | Color |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 19, 18, 17 | O7, O6, O5 | Red, weights 4, 2, 1 |
| 16, 15, 13 | O4, O3, O2 | Green, weights 4, 2, 1 |
| 12, 11 | O1, O0 | Blue, weights 2, 1 |

Pin 14 is ground, so the green bits sit on pins 16, 15, and 13. There is no data pin on pin 14.

Each output can sink about 2 mA and still meet its low level. It is only specified to source 0.4 mA. The LED therefore lights when the bit is low.

For each data pin: +5 V, then 2.2 kohm, then the LED anode. The LED cathode goes to the output pin. A lit LED is a 0. A dark LED is a 1.

| Pin | Name | Place in the byte |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 19 | O7 | Bit 7, left-most bit of the byte |
| 18 | O6 | Bit 6 |
| 17 | O5 | Bit 5 |
| 16 | O4 | Bit 4 |
| 15 | O3 | Bit 3 |
| 13 | O2 | Bit 2 |
| 12 | O1 | Bit 1 |
| 11 | O0 | Bit 0, right-most bit of the byte |

## First four indexes

A5 is on the left of each switch code. A closed switch is 1.

| Index | A5 to A0 | Byte | Lit LEDs | Dark LEDs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 000000 | 0x00 | all eight | none |
| 1 | 000001 | 0x20 | all except pin 17 | pin 17 only |
| 48 | 110000 | 0xFF | none | all eight |
| 63 | 111111 | 0xF3 | pins 15 and 13 | pins 19, 18, 17, 16, 12, 11 |

Index 0 with every LED dark means an address pin from the ground list is high, or CE# or OE# is not at ground. Index 0 with every LED lit, and index 1 dark only on pin 17, means the socket is reading the kit.

## Output-off check

Set index 0, so every LED is lit. Move pin 20 from ground to +5 V. Every LED goes dark, because the outputs turn off and no longer complete the LED path. Return pin 20 to ground. The eight LEDs light again. The same check on pin 22, with pin 20 left at ground, does the same thing.

## Second chip

Power goes off. The first chip comes out. The second chip goes in with its notch at the same end. Power comes back. Index 0, index 1, index 48, and index 63 are the same four rows. Any other row in the table is the same image.
