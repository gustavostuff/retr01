# AT27C256R color PROM

Wire one flashed color PROM, then read the 64 kit bytes on eight LEDs. The burn file is `firmware/a/at27c256r_kit.bin`. A second chip uses the same wires and the same index table.

The package is a 28-pin PDIP with 0.6 inch (600 mil) between the pin rows. The center channel of one breadboard is 0.3 inch, so the two rows do not land on opposite sides of that channel. A 28-pin socket of the same width, with one jumper per pin, keeps the pin numbers on the right holes.

Pin 1 is the pin next to the notch. Down the notched side the pins are 1 through 14. Up the far side the pins are 15 through 28.

Power is off while a chip goes into the socket and while a chip comes out. Pin 1 (VPP) and pin 28 (VCC) share the same 5 V rail, so they rise and fall together. The 12.5 V to 13 V programming pulse stays on the T48.

## 1. Rails

1. Connect the 5 V supply to the breadboard +5 V rail.
2. Connect the supply ground to the breadboard ground rail.
3. Bridge +5 V to the rail on the far side of the breadboard. Bridge ground the same way.

## 2. Seat the socket

Power is still off.

1. Seat the wide socket so each pin lands on its own breadboard row.
2. Seat the PROM in that socket. The notch marks pin 1.

## 3. Power

1. Connect pin 28 (VCC) to +5 V.
2. Connect pin 1 (VPP) to +5 V. Same rail as pin 28.
3. Connect pin 14 (GND) to ground.
4. Connect the 100 nF capacitor from pin 28 to pin 14. Keep the leads short.

## 4. Address pins that stay at ground

The kit occupies addresses 0 through 63. A high on any pin in this list selects some other address. Those locations are still `0xFF`.

1. Connect pin 2 (A12) to ground.
2. Connect pin 3 (A7) to ground.
3. Connect pin 4 (A6) to ground.
4. Connect pin 21 (A10) to ground.
5. Connect pin 23 (A11) to ground.
6. Connect pin 24 (A9) to ground.
7. Connect pin 25 (A8) to ground.
8. Connect pin 26 (A13) to ground.
9. Connect pin 27 (A14) to ground. High on this pin selects the upper 16 KB.

## 5. Enables

Both enables are active low. A high on either one turns the data pins off.

1. Connect pin 20 (CE#) to ground.
2. Connect pin 22 (OE#) to ground.

## 6. Index switches

A5 is the 32s bit. A0 is the 1s bit. The index is the binary value of A5 through A0.

Each of these pins gets a 10 kohm resistor to ground, and a switch to +5 V. A closed switch is a 1. An open switch is a 0, held there by the resistor.

1. Connect a 10 kohm resistor from pin 5 (A5) to ground.
2. Connect a switch from pin 5 to +5 V.
3. Connect a 10 kohm resistor from pin 6 (A4) to ground.
4. Connect a switch from pin 6 to +5 V.
5. Connect a 10 kohm resistor from pin 7 (A3) to ground.
6. Connect a switch from pin 7 to +5 V.
7. Connect a 10 kohm resistor from pin 8 (A2) to ground.
8. Connect a switch from pin 8 to +5 V.
9. Connect a 10 kohm resistor from pin 9 (A1) to ground.
10. Connect a switch from pin 9 to +5 V.
11. Connect a 10 kohm resistor from pin 10 (A0) to ground.
12. Connect a switch from pin 10 to +5 V.

## 7. Data LEDs

The byte is packed R3G3B2, `{RRRGGGBB}`. O7 is the heavy red bit. O0 is the light blue bit.

| Pins | Bits | Color |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 19, 18, 17 | O7, O6, O5 | Red, weights 4, 2, 1 |
| 16, 15, 13 | O4, O3, O2 | Green, weights 4, 2, 1 |
| 12, 11 | O1, O0 | Blue, weights 2, 1 |

Pin 14 is ground, so the green bits sit on pins 16, 15, and 13. There is no data pin on pin 14.

Each output can sink about 2 mA and still meet its low level. It is only specified to source 0.4 mA. The LED therefore lights when the bit is low.

For each data pin the path is +5 V, then 2.2 kohm, then the LED anode. The LED cathode goes to the output pin. A lit LED is a 0. A dark LED is a 1.

1. Connect +5 V to one end of a 2.2 kohm resistor. Connect the other end to an LED anode. Connect that LED cathode to pin 19 (O7).
2. Connect +5 V to one end of a 2.2 kohm resistor. Connect the other end to an LED anode. Connect that LED cathode to pin 18 (O6).
3. Connect +5 V to one end of a 2.2 kohm resistor. Connect the other end to an LED anode. Connect that LED cathode to pin 17 (O5).
4. Connect +5 V to one end of a 2.2 kohm resistor. Connect the other end to an LED anode. Connect that LED cathode to pin 16 (O4).
5. Connect +5 V to one end of a 2.2 kohm resistor. Connect the other end to an LED anode. Connect that LED cathode to pin 15 (O3).
6. Connect +5 V to one end of a 2.2 kohm resistor. Connect the other end to an LED anode. Connect that LED cathode to pin 13 (O2).
7. Connect +5 V to one end of a 2.2 kohm resistor. Connect the other end to an LED anode. Connect that LED cathode to pin 12 (O1).
8. Connect +5 V to one end of a 2.2 kohm resistor. Connect the other end to an LED anode. Connect that LED cathode to pin 11 (O0).

O7 is the left-most bit of the byte. O0 is the right-most bit.

## 8. Power on, then the first four indexes

Turn the 5 V supply on. Switch code is A5 through A0, left to right. A closed switch is 1.

1. Open all six switches. Index 0. All eight LEDs light. The byte is `0x00`.
2. Close only the pin 10 switch. Index 1, code 000001. Pin 17 stays dark. The other seven LEDs stay lit. The byte is `0x20`.
3. Close pin 5 and pin 6. Open pins 7, 8, 9, and 10. Index 48, code 110000. All eight LEDs stay dark. The byte is `0xFF`.
4. Close all six switches. Index 63, code 111111. Pins 15 and 13 light. Pins 19, 18, 17, 16, 12, and 11 stay dark. The byte is `0xF3`.

Index 0 with every LED dark means an address pin from the ground list is high, or CE# or OE# is not at ground. Index 0 with every LED lit, and index 1 dark only on pin 17, means the socket is reading the kit.

## 9. Output-off check

1. Open all six switches, so every LED is lit.
2. Move pin 20 from ground to +5 V. Every LED goes dark. The outputs are off, so they no longer complete the LED path.
3. Return pin 20 to ground. The eight LEDs light again.
4. Move pin 22 from ground to +5 V, with pin 20 left at ground. Every LED goes dark again.
5. Return pin 22 to ground. The eight LEDs light again.

## 11. Second chip

1. Turn the 5 V supply off.
2. Lift the first chip out. Notch end stays the same for the next chip.
3. Seat the second chip. Notch at the same end.
4. Turn the 5 V supply on.
5. Repeat index 0, index 1, index 48, and index 63. The same four results mean the second chip holds the same kit.

Any other row in the index table is the same image on both chips.
